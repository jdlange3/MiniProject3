# Validation Reflection: ttb615

I validated ten of amart170's MiniProject2 projects across five fields (50 records). Before discussion we agreed on 25 of the 50. Currentstatus matched on 10/10, HasRecovered on 8/10, WhyRecovered on 5/10, ActivityPattern on 2/10, and RecentThemes on none, because that field was missing from the author's stats file. All 25 disagreements were resolved in favor of my values after Adam reviewed the commit evidence. The low ActivityPattern agreement came from the same issue: "cyclical" was applied to projects whose yearly commit counts show decline or uneven bursts rather than a repeating interval. The adjudicated labels are declining, irregular and declining for those three.

Looking at the adjudicated labels together, the gaps were usually preceded by a stabilization or "finished" signal. 'darkchem' added its paper citation and then went quiet. 'nglview' finished a run of widget and typing refactors before its 2025 pause. 'fzenke/auryn' made version fixes and removed its MPI bindings before 14 months of silence. 'uwdata/falcon' added a README note that much of its functionality now lives in other libraries. Recovery had three kinds of triggers: external pressure such as Python 3.13 compatibility for 'nglview' and CRAN warnings for 'spacetime', new people, like outside contributors who added features and tests to 'pubmed_parser' in 2024 and a contributor who brought CI automation and refactors to 'openmarlaria' in 2022; and build or infrastructure work, as in 'fresnel' after its 2017 pause.

Gaps did not mean project death in a few cases. 'fresnel' paused for four months in 2017 and then produced over 1,200 commits. 'openmarlaria' (nine months) and 'pubmed_parser' (ten months) both resumed with sustained work. 'darkchem' and 'auryn' looked recovered only on paper. One doc commit after 31 months, and three housekeeping commits on a single day, followed by silence. 'falcon' has only dependabot bumps since mid-2025, and interproscan's post-gap commits are end-of-support notices, so its "recovery" is actually a wind-down. Commits were not the issue here, but just a sign that there was human activity going on in the project. 

The main interpretation pitfalls were using "cyclical" for any project with several quiet periods; counting any new commit, including bot bumps and one-day housekeeping, as recovery; explaining a recovery from what the project is known for instead of the commits right after that specific gap; and mixing up Currentstatus dates, the data runs through October 2026 which would be before the cutoff point. We resolved the first three by going back to the yearly commit counts and the first commits after each gap, and by treating the codebook definitions as the tiebreaker. The fourth caused no disagreement, but it is worth flagging. Once we looked at the same evidence, we agreed on everything.
